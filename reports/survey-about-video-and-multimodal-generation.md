# Survey on Video and Multimodal Generation: Architectures, Breakthroughs, and Open Problems

## TL;DR
- Modern video and multimodal generation is increasingly driven by Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), state space models (SSMs), and unified autoregressive frameworks that handle text, video, audio, and action within shared token spaces [1][2][3][4].
- Core architectural paradigms combine spatial-temporal patchification with specialized diffusion or autoregressive inference engines (such as Open-Sora, Lumina-T2X, and VideoAR) to scale resolution, duration, and cross-modal flexibility [5][6][7].
- Recent advances focus heavily on post-training alignment, trajectory-aware preference optimization (Diffusion-APO), block-diffusion long-video generation (Inferix), and goal-directed video world models (WorldGuide) [8][9][10][11].
- Major open challenges remain in physical reasoning limitations (evaluated via IPV-Bench and the World Embedding Benchmark), safety alignment against deepfakes, and managing quadratic computational complexities [12][13][14][15][16].

## Background
The field of generative artificial intelligence has expanded rapidly from single-image synthesis to complex video and multimodal generation, where systems model continuous spatial-temporal dynamics and cross-modal interactions. Foundational work established the feasibility of latent diffusion models, such as Latte, which adapt Transformer blocks directly into latent spaces to capture video distributions [1]. However, standard attention mechanisms face severe quadratic complexity bottlenecks when scaling to high-resolution, long-duration video sequences [2]. To address this, recent architectures integrate state space models (SSMs) like Diffusion Mamba (DiM) and hybrid SSM-Transformer structures, successfully balancing long-range dependency modeling with computational tractability [2][3]. Furthermore, comprehensive industry and academic surveys analyze compute scaling laws, dataset curation challenges, and hardware requirements for training billion-parameter video models [16]. Conditioning mechanisms have also evolved from simple text prompts to intricate multi-signal controls.

## Architectural Families and Scaling Paradigms
Video generation architectures span several distinct model families and scaling paradigms, reflecting different trade-offs between sample quality, inference speed, and multimodality. Spatial-Temporal Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) coupled with 3D autoencoders form the bedrock of many high-fidelity video generators, as demonstrated by Open-Sora and flow-based large diffusion transformers like Lumina-T2X that scale seamlessly across arbitrary modalities, resolutions, and temporal durations [5][6]. Concurrently, unified autoregressive multimodal frameworks such as Unified-IO 2 tokenize diverse inputs—including vision, text, audio, and action—into a shared space, processed entirely through standard transformer backbones [4]. For purely generative video workflows, large-scale visual autoregressive approaches like VideoAR employ spatiotemporal cubes and multi-scale next-frame prediction to enhance computational efficiency and temporal consistency [7].

## Recent Breakthroughs and Advanced Techniques
Over the last two years (2024–2026), the video generation landscape has witnessed remarkable breakthroughs in post-training alignment, long-video generation, and physical realism [8]. Post-training alignment for video generation presents unique hurdles, including error accumulation over time and motion-appearance coupling [8]. To resolve discrepancies between training noise distributions and practical inference trajectories, algorithms like Diffusion-APO introduce trajectory-aware direct preference alignment for video diffusion transformers [9]. For long-video generation and interactive world simulation, semi-autoregressive inference engines like Inferix utilize block-diffusion to generate video tokens in blocks, achieving efficient long-sequence synthesis [10]. Moreover, recent Hugging Face daily papers introduce goal-directed video world models such as WorldGuide, which formulate procedural video generation as a closed-loop system where video world models adapt generation outcomes to execution results [11]. Rigorous evaluation paradigms have also emerged, including ProPhy for progressive physical alignment [17], and the World Embedding Benchmark, which assesses physical fidelity across 8,000 controlled simulation cases spanning fluid mechanics, solid mechanics, dynamics, and optics [12].

## Trends and Open Problems
Despite rapid progress, video and multimodal generation faces critical open problems, safety risks, and compute bottlenecks [16]. Safety challenges remain paramount; benchmarks like T2VSafetyBench and studies on unsafe video generation highlight hazardous content risks and the delicate trade-off between safety guardrails and model usability [13][18]. The proliferation of synthetic media has intensified the tug-of-war between sophisticated deepfake video generation and forensic detection techniques [14]. Beyond safety, physical reasoning and world consistency remain fundamental limitations, as evidenced by IPV-Bench ("Impossible Videos"), which reveals that current models struggle with basic physical laws and semantic consistency [15]. Finally, looking toward future directions, researchers are increasingly treating video generation not merely as an aesthetic medium, but as a unified interface and "new language" for real-world decision making in robotics, autonomous driving, and interactive agent systems [19].
