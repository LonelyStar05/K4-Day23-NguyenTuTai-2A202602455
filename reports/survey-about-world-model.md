# World Models: Definitions, Architectures, Benchmarks and Open Problems

## TL;DR

- A world model is a learned predictive model of environment dynamics — an internal simulator whose rollouts support perception, prediction and decision-making — but the field still has no consensus definition, split between "understanding the world" and "predicting the future" [1][2].
- Two technical families dominate: latent, planning-oriented models that roll out inside a learned representation (MuZero, DreamerV3, TD-MPC2, the JEPA line) [3][4][5][6], and generative video world models that render the future in pixels (Sora, Genie, GameNGen, Cosmos) [7][8][9][10].
- In the last two years the generative branch became real-time and interactive: Genie 3 navigates photorealistic 720p worlds at 24 FPS with roughly a minute of visual memory [11], Matrix-Game 3.0 reports up to 40 FPS at 720p [12], and ABot-World-0 streams 720p at 16 FPS on a single desktop GPU [13].
- Evaluation has moved beyond perceptual quality to physics adherence, control alignment and long-horizon consistency; the diagnostics are damning — physics win rates correlate only 0.28 with VBench [14], the best model recovers physical parameters at 0.45 mIoU [15], and long-horizon planning scores 17.27 with inverse-dynamics success near 0% for most video foundation models [16].
- The core unsolved problems are error accumulation over long rollouts, counterfactual/physical generalization, object-interaction fidelity that resists 100x data scaling, and whether pixel generators are world models at all [17][18][1][2].

## Background

A world model, in the sense used across the surveyed literature, is an internal model of the environment that an agent can roll forward. The concept is usually traced to Marvin Minsky's frame representation in the 1960s and to the psychological theory of mental models; Ha and Schmidhuber (2018) revived and popularised the term with neural networks that learn compressed spatial and temporal representations of RL environments [1][19]. Their model is an encoder plus a dynamics component: the agent is trained entirely inside a hallucinated dream generated by the world model and the policy is then transferred back into the real environment [19][20]. The companion paper showed the same recipe trained by evolution reaching state-of-the-art results across several environments [20].

The standard formalisation that grew out of this is a Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM), where an encoder maps the observation $x_t$ to a stochastic representation $z_t$, a recurrent sequence model predicts the sequence given past actions with hidden state $h_t$, and the pair $(h_t, z_t)$ forms the state from which rewards and continuation flags are predicted and inputs are reconstructed [4]. PlaNet established the core technical obstacle set that still defines the field: model inaccuracy, accumulating multi-step prediction errors, multimodal futures, and overconfident predictions off the training distribution [21].

A second, competing formalisation comes from LeCun's position paper on autonomous machine intelligence: a configurable predictive world model implemented as a joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) that predicts representations rather than inputs, with hierarchical JEPA for long-horizon planning and uncertainty handled by encoder invariance and a latent variable $z$ [22]. The ACM Computing Surveys overview frames the field explicitly around these two functions — constructing internal representations to understand the mechanism of the world versus predicting future states to simulate and guide decisions — and states that the definition of a world model remains an ongoing debate [1]. A recent perspective article agrees that there is no consensus on what a world model fundamentally is, what it should predict, or how it should be built, and proposes the definition of a world model as a finite-resource approximation to the state-transition processes of the physical world, emphasising omnimodality, multidimensional asynchronicity and locality [2]. The urgency is practical: physical AI is argued to need world foundation models as digital twins to scale interleaved observation-action data [10].

## Theme 1: Latent representation versus generative simulation

The clearest fault line in the literature is what the model should predict. LeCun argues that generative reconstruction is never the right objective and that joint-embedding architectures predicting in latent space suffice [2][22]; in JEPA, two encoders produce representations $s_x$ and $s_y$ and a predictor maps one to the other, with energy defined as prediction error in representation space, so that unpredictable detail is discarded by encoder invariance [22]. I-JEPA instantiates this for images and is strikingly efficient — a ViT-Huge/14 trained on ImageNet with 16 A100s in under 72 hours — while avoiding pixel reconstruction and hand-crafted augmentations [23].

The value-equivalent branch makes the opposite trade in a different way: MuZero predicts only the quantities needed by tree search — reward, policy and value — with no observation reconstruction at all [3]. Reconstruction-based imagination agents keep the decoder, arguing it keeps representations informative: DreamerV3 predicts rewards and episode continuation flags and reconstructs inputs [4][24]. TD-MPC2 sits between them with an implicit, decoder-free, task-oriented latent used for model-predictive control [5]. A study of the JEPA-WM family characterises which design choices actually matter — model architecture, training objective and planning algorithm — and reports outperforming the DINO-WM and V-JEPA-2-AC baselines in navigation and manipulation once those choices are made well [25].

The perspective article that reviews these positions also offers a functional taxonomy — renderers that generate pixels, simulators that predict state transitions, planners that propose actions — and then critiques it as classifying decodings of a representation rather than the representation itself; it places Sora and Seedance in the observation-level renderer region, Genie 3 as observation-level but moving toward 3D world representation, and JEPA and Dreamer in the latent column, with MuZero in a task-aligned space optimised only for reward and value [2].

## Theme 2: Generative video world models and real-time interactivity

Sora is the reference point for the generative view: a diffusion transformer over "spacetime patches" of video latents, trained jointly on images and videos of variable duration and resolution, generating up to a minute of video and reporting emergent 3D consistency, long-range coherence and object permanence — but only qualitative results, and clear failures such as glass shattering and incorrect state changes like eating food [7].

Making these models interactive required action conditioning and memory. Genie learned, without any action labels, from roughly 200,000 hours of public Internet gaming video: an 11B model with a spatiotemporal tokenizer, an autoregressive dynamics model and a latent action model, controllable frame by frame and scaling gracefully from 40M to 2.7B parameters, but limited to 16 frames of memory, hallucinating futures, and running at about 1 FPS [8]. GameNGen turned DOOM into the first game engine powered entirely by a neural model, running at 20 FPS on a single TPU with next-frame PSNR 29.4, and human raters barely better than chance at distinguishing clips from the real game after five minutes of autoregressive generation [9]. Genie 3 generalises this to text-prompted, photorealistic 720p worlds at 24 FPS, where consistency and visual memory (recalling up to a minute back) are emergent rather than backed by any explicit 3D representation, with the acknowledged limit of a few minutes of continuous interaction [11].

Open and industrial systems converge on the same engineering pattern: distill for speed, add memory for consistency. Matrix-Game 2.0 generates minute-level video at 25 FPS using few-step autoregressive diffusion, an action injection module for frame-level mouse and keyboard control, and about 1,200 hours of Unreal Engine and GTA5 data [26]; Matrix-Game 3.0 adds camera-aware memory retrieval, models prediction residuals so the model learns self-correction, and reaches up to 40 FPS at 720p with distribution-matching distillation and quantization [12]. Hunyuan-GameCraft unifies keyboard and mouse inputs in a shared camera representation with hybrid history conditioning, trained on over one million gameplay recordings from 100+ AAA games [27]; GameGen-X is a diffusion transformer for open-world game video with an InstructNet that injects multimodal control while the foundation model stays frozen [28]. On the foundation-model axis, Cosmos introduces the World Foundation Model concept with both diffusion and autoregressive transformers over continuous and discrete video latents and open weights [10]; Cosmos-Predict2.5 is flow-based, unifies Text2World/Image2World/Video2World, is trained on 200M curated clips and released at 2B and 14B [29]; Cosmos 3 extends the family to an omnimodal mixture-of-transformers processing language, image, video, audio and action [30].

## Theme 3: Latent world models for planning and control

Model-based RL supplies the strongest evidence that latent world models work as decision-making engines. DreamerV2 already learned behaviours purely from predictions in the compact latent space of a world model, addressing Atari modelling that had long remained open, and DreamerV2 previously surpassed human performance on Atari [31][4]. DreamerV3 trains an RSSM world model, a critic and an actor from replayed experience and improves behaviour by imagining future scenarios; robustness techniques (observation symlog, KL balancing with free bits, unimix, percentile return normalisation, symexp two-hot losses) let a single fixed configuration outperform specialised methods on over 150 tasks, and it was the first algorithm to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch without human data or curricula, with each agent trained on one A100 [4][24]. MuZero combines MCTS with a learned model and matches AlphaZero on Go, chess and shogi while reaching a new state of the art on 57 Atari games without access to game rules [3]. EfficientZero pushed the same lineage into the low-data regime with 194.3% mean and 109.0% median human-normalised performance on Atari 100k [32], and EfficientZero V2 later reports super-human performance on Atari 100k within about two hours of gameplay and superior outcomes on 50 of 66 evaluated tasks relative to DreamerV3, while its tree search consumes far fewer imagined latent states than TD-MPC2's MPPI planning [33]. TD-MPC2 plans by local trajectory optimisation in a decoder-free latent space, improving over baselines on 104 online RL tasks with one hyperparameter set and scaling to a single 317M-parameter agent performing 80 tasks across domains, embodiments and action spaces [5].

The JEPA family targets the same planning goal in representation space: V-JEPA 2 is pretrained on more than 1 million hours of internet video, then post-trained into an action-conditioned latent world model (V-JEPA 2-AC) with less than 62 hours of unlabeled Droid robot video for robotic planning [6], and JEPA-WM shows that planning directly in the learned representation can beat established latent baselines [25]. The contrast is architectural rather than philosophical: Dreamer plans by imagination with a decoder-backed state [4], MuZero and TD-MPC2 plan with value- and task-oriented latents [3][5], and JEPA plans by predicting future embeddings [6][25].

## Theme 4: Embodied and robotic world models

Physical robots are where the latent branch has been validated outside simulation. DayDreamer applied Dreamer online to four physical robots with no simulator at all and with the same hyperparameters throughout: an A1 quadruped learned to roll off its back, stand and walk from scratch in one hour and adapted to pushes within ten minutes, two arms learned pick-and-place from camera images and sparse rewards, and a wheeled robot learned goal reaching from images [34]. V-JEPA 2-AC demonstrates the same for latent planning with a small amount of interaction data [6], and Navigation World Models use a conditional diffusion transformer of up to 1B parameters inside a model-predictive control loop that optimises actions with the cross-entropy method to match a goal image; CDiT's cost is linear in context frames with about 4x fewer FLOPs than a standard DiT, supports constraint-aware planning, and transfers zero-shot to unseen environments from a single image [35]. RoboDreamer addresses compositional generalisation of text-to-video imagination by decomposing instructions into lower-level primitives [36].

The current frontier is the "world-action model": world models that serve directly as robot policies. Long-WAM's central finding is that access to history is not the same as using it — longer histories pay off far more when the video foundation was pretrained autoregressively [37]. RoboJEPA attacks the missing scaling theory for latent world models, training across 12 embodiments and finding that imagination error follows a second-order power law in compute [38]. A survey of world models for robot learning notes that the literature is still fragmented across architectures, functional roles and application domains, while robotic video world models have progressed from imagination-based generation to controllable, structured, foundation-scale formulations, and summarises datasets and evaluation protocols [39]. On the data side, Open X-Embodiment pooled 60 datasets into over 1M real robot trajectories across 22 embodiments and 527 skills, with RT-1-X improving success rates by 50% over prior collaborators' best methods and RT-2-X showing roughly 3x better generalisation than a model trained only on the evaluation embodiment [40].

## Theme 5: Benchmarks and evaluation

Video world-model evaluation has specialised in the last two years. WorldModelBench evaluates video generation models as world models on 350 image+text conditions spanning 7 application domains, scoring instruction following and physics adherence with five binary physical laws and crowd-sourcing 67K human labels over 14 frontier models; a fine-tuned 2B judger predicts violations 8.6% more accurately than GPT-4o, and — the key diagnostic result — frame-wise quality win rates correlate 0.69 with VBench while physics-adherence win rates correlate only 0.28 with the VBench total score [14]. WorldBench argues prior physics benchmarks are entangled and instead isolates one law or concept per clip, using 469 intuitive-physics videos with ground-truth segmentations and a parameter-estimation suite for gravity, friction and viscosity where models must recover values such as 9.81 m/s²; the strongest model, Cosmos-1, reaches 0.45 mIoU, models fail on gravity, and mIoU correlates with human motion-quality judgements at r = 0.846 [15]. PhyGround uses 250 curated prompts with expected physical outcomes and 13 laws, collecting 5,796 annotations and over 37.4K labels from 459 annotators, and releases PhyJudge-9B with 3.3% aggregate relative bias versus 16.6% for Gemini-3.1-Pro [41]. WorldSimBench separates explicit perceptual evaluation, using a Human Preference Evaluator trained on fine-grained human feedback, from implicit manipulative evaluation that tests whether generated video is actionable as control signals [42].

Interactivity adds its own measurement problem. WBench proposes a multi-turn benchmark with 289 test cases and 1,058 interaction turns across navigation, subject action, event editing and perspective switching, scored by 22 automatic sub-metrics validated against human judgements, and finds that among 20 state-of-the-art models none performs strongly across all dimensions [43]. WorldMark observes that interactive models such as Genie, YUME, HY-World and Matrix-Game are each evaluated on private scenes and trajectories, so it supplies a unified WASD-style action-mapping layer, 500 evaluation cases across viewpoints and three difficulty tiers, a modular toolkit for visual quality, control alignment and world consistency, and a public comparison arena [44]. For embodied world models, WoW-World-Eval builds an Embodied Turing Test on 609 robot manipulation data items with a 22-metric protocol whose overall score correlates above 0.93 with human preference, and reports only 17.27 on long-horizon planning, at best 68.02 on physical consistency, and inverse-dynamics-model success collapsing to approximately 0% for most models against 40.74% for its own reference [16]. Control benchmarks remain dataset-based: the Atari 100k protocol covers 26 games and about 2 hours of gameplay with human-normalised scoring, alongside DMControl for continuous control [33][32], and robot learning relies on large trajectory corpora such as Open X-Embodiment [40].

## Trends and open problems

What changed in the last two years is the shift from single-domain latent agents to foundation-scale, interactive, real-time world models. Latent planning agents became general algorithms [4][5], video generators became controllable simulators [8][9], and world foundation models were framed as infrastructure for physical AI [10][29][30]. Real-time interactivity is now the headline metric: 24 FPS at 720p for a closed model [11], 40 FPS at 720p for an open one [12], and 720p at 16 FPS on a single consumer desktop GPU with 1.2s action-to-first-frame latency [13]. The enabling recipe — causal/streaming architectures plus distillation — is repeated across Matrix-Game 2.0 and 3.0, Hunyuan-GameCraft and ABot-World-0 [26][12][27][13], and the newest framing is the world-action model that couples visual history directly to control [37][39].

Several problems look structural rather than incremental. First, error accumulation: world models must achieve long-horizon temporal consistency while mitigating drift [45], and a dedicated study shows that small errors accumulate until trajectories become physically implausible, with symplectic integration of a learned energy keeping rollouts bounded for up to 100x the training horizon while equal-capacity predictors, energy-regularised predictors and tuned neural ODEs diverge [17]. Second, counterfactual generalisation: encoding physical coupling through an explicit linear factorisation lets a model follow a never-seen sign of that coupling, whereas unrestricted parameterisations stay locked to the training law [17], matching the survey-level warning that capturing causal structure and reasoning about counterfactuals is the field's first named open problem [2]. Third, data scaling is not a cure-all: increasing ego-centric training video by 100x improves agent modelling while object interaction fidelity remains far lower and improves slowly [18], and unified datasets are still scarce [45]. Fourth, evaluation remains the bottleneck — physics adherence is nearly orthogonal to perceptual quality [14], parameter recovery is far from physical constants [15], no interactive model is strong on all dimensions [43], and cross-model comparison was impossible until shared action interfaces appeared [44].

Finally, the definitional dispute is unresolved and consequential. It is openly questioned whether models such as Sora truly qualify as comprehensive world models [1], the renderer/simulator/planner taxonomy is contested for classifying decodings rather than representations [2], and latent-prediction advocates maintain that generative reconstruction is the wrong objective at all [22][2], while others argue reconstruction quality remains the best way to trace information a compressed representation fails to preserve [2]. The trade-off between model capability and the computational efficiency needed for real-time control [45], and the gap between generated video and real-world actionability that near-zero inverse-dynamics success exposes [16], are the practical face of that dispute.
